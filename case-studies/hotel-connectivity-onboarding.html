Case study · End-to-end implementation

Turning hotel inventory into a reliable live booking flow.

A sanitized account of how I coordinated hotel, commercial, database, and technology stakeholders to translate externally published room and rate-plan data into a tested, saleable configuration.

Role
Implementation owner
Domain
Hotel connectivity
Core work
Mapping · UAT · Go-live
Environment
Cross-functional, multilingual

The challenge

The hotel had published inventory, but that did not yet make it commercially usable.

The external platform exposed multiple room and meal-plan combinations using hotel-facing names and identifiers. Internally, those combinations had to be matched to the correct room concepts, service lines, pricing logic, and sales priorities before reservations could be trusted.

The risk was not simply a failed connection. A technically available product could still be wrong for the business: duplicated rooms, mismatched meal plans, misleading availability, incorrect prices, or reservations delivered against the wrong product definition.

Operational ambiguity

Hotel naming and internal product naming did not align one-to-one.

Multiple owners

Hotel, contracting, product, database, operations, and connectivity teams each owned part of the outcome.

Production risk

A mapping that looked correct in configuration still required end-to-end reservation validation.

My responsibility

I owned the translation layer between partner inventory and the internal booking product.

Analyse

  • Review rooms, plans, identifiers, inclusions, and availability
  • Compare partner data with internal product definitions
  • Identify duplicates, gaps, and ambiguous combinations

Coordinate

  • Clarify intended inventory with the hotel
  • Request internal room or service-line changes
  • Align commercial relevance with contracting and product teams

Validate

  • Configure mappings and business rules
  • Run searches, reservations, modifications where applicable, and cancellations
  • Confirm delivery and launch readiness with all parties

Implementation flow

A controlled path from source data to live distribution.

01Inventory review

Rooms, plans, codes, inclusions, and constraints.

02Business mapping

Match inventory to internal products and sales priorities.

03Configuration

Create or adjust room concepts, service lines, and mappings.

04End-to-end UAT

Search, price, reserve, verify delivery, and cancel.

05Go-live control

Document status, confirm ownership, and monitor initial flow.

Key decisions

The difficult work was deciding what should be sold—not merely what could be mapped.

Reduce unnecessary complexity.

Where several external combinations represented the same internal room concept, I worked toward a smaller, clearer product set rather than reproducing every hotel-facing variation.

Separate availability from correctness.

A room appearing in search was treated as one test result, not proof of a complete implementation. Price, occupancy, inclusions, reservation delivery, and cancellation all had to align.

Escalate with evidence.

When expected and actual behaviour differed, I documented identifiers, dates, product combinations, screenshots, reservation references, and reproduction steps so technical teams could investigate efficiently.

Testing approach

UAT covered the commercial and technical journey.

Search & availability

Verified that intended rooms and plans appeared for controlled dates and occupancy combinations.

Pricing

Compared external and internal outputs, including markups, fees, taxes, and known rate behaviour.

Reservation delivery

Confirmed that test bookings generated the expected downstream record and reached the relevant partner flow.

Cancellation

Validated cancellation handling and checked that temporary test inventory was cleaned up correctly.

Exception handling

Logged missing plans, inconsistent availability, mapping gaps, and price discrepancies with reproducible evidence.

Launch readiness

Separated confirmed items, open dependencies, and post-launch monitoring responsibilities.

Outcome

A configuration that internal teams could sell and partners could support.

  • Converted partner-published inventory into a controlled internal product structure
  • Resolved room and plan ambiguity before it became a customer-facing issue
  • Coordinated required changes across technical and commercial stakeholders
  • Completed reservation and cancellation testing before launch approval
  • Created a traceable implementation record for support and future maintenance

Confidentiality note: hotel names, identifiers, commercial terms, screenshots, and internal configuration details have been removed or generalized. This case study describes my working method and responsibilities without disclosing partner-sensitive information.
