Case study · Production troubleshooting

Diagnosing a booking connection when every stakeholder saw a different truth.

A sanitized account of how I investigated inconsistent integration behaviour, separated system responsibilities, gathered reproducible evidence, and kept hotel, platform, and technical teams aligned.

Role
Technical coordinator
Domain
Hotel connectivity
Core work
Reproduction · Evidence · Escalation
Environment
Multi-vendor integration

The challenge

The connection appeared disabled to one party, yet searches and bookings still worked.

The hotel and external platform did not share the same understanding of the connection state. Earlier testing suggested that the route had been disabled, but later searches returned rooms and new reservations could again be created.

The issue could not be reduced to “the interface works” or “the interface is broken.” A booking passed through several independent systems, and each stakeholder controlled only part of that chain.

Conflicting observations

Historical behaviour, current search results, and the hotel’s understanding did not align.

Layered ownership

The booking platform, external adaptor, channel manager, and hotel PMS had separate responsibilities.

Operational urgency

Live availability created commercial risk while the receiving and processing path remained uncertain.

System model

I mapped the flow before assigning blame.

01Internal booking platform

Searches, prices, and creates the reservation request.

02External connection adaptor

Transforms and transmits the request to the provider.

03Channel or CRS layer

Controls distributed inventory and booking messages.

04Hotel-side interface

Routes the message toward the property environment.

05PMS / hotel operations

Receives and processes the reservation operationally.

This model prevented a common troubleshooting error: treating the hotel PMS as if it were the direct endpoint of the internal booking platform.

My approach

I converted an ambiguous operational complaint into a structured technical case.

Reproduce

  • Search controlled dates and room combinations
  • Create a traceable test reservation
  • Record expected and actual behaviour at each step

Compare

  • Contrast current behaviour with earlier test results
  • Separate availability, booking creation, delivery, and PMS receipt
  • Identify which conclusions were proven and which were assumptions

Escalate

  • Provide reservation references and timestamps
  • Share the relevant outbound booking message where available
  • Ask each technical owner to verify its segment of the chain

Evidence package

The escalation was designed to reduce another round of vague questions.

Reproduction context

Property, test date, occupancy, room or service-line reference, and the exact action performed.

Behavioural evidence

Search result, price response, reservation identifier, cancellation state, and screenshots where useful.

Technical evidence

Relevant outbound reservation request, timestamps, interface references, and the distinction between adaptor, provider, and PMS responsibilities.

Decision request

A precise question for each owner: confirm whether the connection is enabled, where the message was accepted, and what the hotel should verify downstream.

Communication control

I kept technical uncertainty from becoming stakeholder confusion.

With the hotel

Explained what had been proven on our side without claiming visibility into systems we did not control.

With platform support

Shared a reproducible case and requested confirmation of adaptor and provider behaviour.

With internal teams

Separated confirmed functionality from open risk so commercial teams did not overinterpret a successful search.

Outcome

The investigation established a reliable shared picture of the booking path.

  • Confirmed that current search and reservation creation were functioning
  • Clarified that the external adaptor communicated with the provider rather than directly with the hotel PMS
  • Produced evidence the hotel could use with its own provider or PMS contact
  • Reduced circular escalation by assigning verification to the correct system owners
  • Documented the difference between technical connectivity and confirmed hotel-side processing

Confidentiality note: hotel names, reservation identifiers, payload content, provider names, screenshots, and internal support correspondence have been removed or generalized.
